# Verdicts — where to go hard

Use this after the design read. Pick the row that matches the brief. The hard layer is a ceiling, not a shopping list.

## Product marketing / SaaS landing

**Audience:** buyers who already know the category. They need to feel the product, not be told it is "intelligent."

**May go hard:** `product-state` first. Put a real (or honestly miniaturized) product in the first viewport, the way Linear and Amie do. Type can be large, but it is not the event. Layout can be wide and dark or light, but chrome stays thin.

**Must hold back:** invented illustration, bento-of-everything, three-tier pricing as the visual climax, logo walls inside the hero, motion that hides the product.

**Aesthetic:** quiet confidence. Specific headline. One accent at most. The page should still work if you turn motion off.

**Wrong-hard:** a cinematic WebGL hero on top of a product that is a list and a text box. That is costume.

## Brand / studio / campaign site

**Audience:** clients, press, talent. They are hiring a point of view.

**May go hard:** `type`, `layout`, or `motion` — pick one. AndAgain goes hard on type (the wordmark is the architecture). Offer Studio goes hard on motion-as-chapter (burst world, then a white work stage). Both leave the other layers quiet.

**Must hold back:** case-study cards that all look like Dribbble shots, agency clichés (rotated index labels, "scroll to explore" as decoration, locale/weather strips), a second climax after the first.

**Aesthetic:** one graphic idea taken to the wall. The rest of the site is a straight corridor.

**Wrong-hard:** burst background plus huge type plus coverflow plus scroll-hijack plus grain plus a second animated footer. Two climaxes cancel.

## Personal / designer / applicant portfolio

**Audience:** a human who will spend two minutes deciding if this person is specific.

**May go hard:** `image` or a single metaphor, or `type` if the person is a writer. imjane.top goes hard on one image-metaphor (a tree you pick apples from). ped.ro goes hard on type-as-interface (words are the controls). kons.fyi goes hard on emptiness (one floating island).

**Must hold back:** chrome, nav weight, "About / Work / Contact" templates, skill-bar charts, 12-project grids, fake case-study framing. The person is the design. The site is a frame.

**Aesthetic:** more restrained than marketing. Personality over spectacle. If you removed the name, you should still know whose site it is.

**Wrong-hard:** a student site that looks like an Awwwards agency reel. That reads as costume, not character.

## Agency portfolio (studio showing client work)

**Audience:** other studios and brand clients.

**May go hard:** `layout` or `image`. Work should be large, sequenced, and uneven. Names of clients can be quiet; the work should not be.

**Must hold back:** capabilities lists as six icons, "we are a digital partner" filler, equal tiles.

**Aesthetic:** black/white or one material. Type can bleed. Work is photographed or captured, not mocked up as generic 13-inch laptops unless the laptop is a designed object (Offer Studio's coverflow is a designed object; a grey iMac mock is not).

## Editorial / magazine / essay

**May go hard:** `type` and reading rhythm. Measure, crop, pull quotes.

**Must hold back:** cards, pills, bento, hero video.

## E-commerce / product story

**May go hard:** `image`. The object is the design. Lighting, crop, scale.

**Must hold back:** the beige-and-brass "artisan" default, spec tables with a line under every row, lifestyle copy that could sell any object.

## Community / education / event

**May go hard:** `layout` (a dense but authored grid) or `type` (a poster).

**Must hold back:** dashboard chrome, course-catalog cards in batches of three.

## Product app / dashboard / settings / docs

**Hard layer = none.** This skill mostly stands down.

Spacing, type hierarchy, empty states, and focus rings still matter. Cinematic heroes, scroll hijacks, and display serifs do not. If a marketing site and an app share a shell, keep the app quieter than the site.

## Redesign of an existing site

**Preserve:** extract tokens, IA, and voice first. Hard layer may rise by one notch, not a full costume change.

**Overhaul:** treat visuals as greenfield, keep content and URLs. Still one hard layer.

If you cannot tell which, ask once: preserve the brand, or start visually from scratch?

---

## How "hard" is allowed to look

| Layer | Hard means | Hard does not mean |
|---|---|---|
| layout | One structural idea: bleed, split, coverflow, empty field | Every section a new grid stunt |
| type | One family doing scale and crop; words as UI | A second display face for "emphasis" |
| material | One surface: paper, night, glass, metal | Glass on every card |
| motion | One chapter change, or physics on the primary control | Fade-up on every block |
| image | One real picture or one 3D metaphor doing the storytelling | Stock plus gradient orbs |
| product-state | The actual UI, interactive, in the first screen | A div-built fake dashboard |

If two layers both feel necessary, keep the stronger one and demote the other to a whisper.
